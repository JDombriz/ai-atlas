# Stage 1 — Professional use


<img src="../../assets/bandas/banda-e1-indice-en.svg" alt="Stage 1 · Professional use" width="560">

> English translation of the Spanish original: [README.md](README.md). Spanish is the canonical text; this version follows the revision of 22 October 2026.

> **Objective:** work with models as a daily tool, with judgement to choose them, to give them context and to know what each use costs.

Stage 0 taught what is meant when artificial intelligence is spoken of and, above all, when it is not needed. This stage answers the next question, the one that arrives on Monday morning: **with six models available and prices that differ a hundredfold, which one is used for this, what do you give it, and how much is it going to cost**.

What is not learned here is how to build systems. What is learned is how to use well a tool that is already installed, that is rarely used well and that is billed by the unit.

## By the end of this stage, you will be able to

- Choose a model for a specific task and defend the choice with a test of your own, not with a leaderboard.
- Write a reusable instruction: with its context, its output format and an explicit criterion for «this is right».
- Decide what information the model is given, in what order, and what is deliberately left out.
- Estimate the cost of a repeated use before promising it, including the small print that doubles it.
- Recognise when the model is not the tool and the work is done another way.

## Prerequisite

Stage 0 closed, with its exercise handed in. Nothing more of the subject is assumed than what was seen there. The craft of programming is taken as known at the level of the [path's starting point](../00-punto-de-partida.md): editor, terminal, Git and some Python or JavaScript. The pages of this stage do not require writing code; the test bench in the [recipe](../../recetas/banco-de-diez-casos.en.md) does ask for a while at the terminal or a spreadsheet.

## Exit criterion

Choose a model for a task of your own, justify it with a test of at least ten cases, write the instruction that task needs and estimate what it would cost to run a thousand times. The exercise that closes the stage —with its template— is published with pages 4 and 5.

## Pages

1. [How to choose a model for a task](01-como-elegir-un-modelo.en.md) — the five variables that decide, the order they are looked at in, the small print that doubles the bill and the test that settles the doubt.
2. [The instruction: asking well and getting something usable](02-la-instruccion.en.md) — the five parts of an instruction that works, what is fixed once and what is asked each time, and a before and after on a dull task.
3. [The context: what it is given and in what order](03-el-contexto.en.md) — why the model does not remember, what a token budget is, how what you give it is ordered and when to start from scratch.
4. The cost of a repeated use — *in preparation*.
5. The limits: when the model is not the tool — *in preparation*.

## What this stage does not cover

Document retrieval, persistent memory, step graphs and systematic evaluation: that is Stage 2. Agentic patterns, tools and self-repair: Stage 3. What is learned here is how to use a single model well, which is 80 % of real use and the basis of everything else.
