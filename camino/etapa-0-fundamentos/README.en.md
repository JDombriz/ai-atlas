# Stage 0 — Foundations


<img src="../../assets/bandas/banda-indice-en.svg" alt="Stage 0" width="560">
> English translation of the Spanish original: [README.md](README.md). Spanish is the canonical text; this version follows the revision of 10 October 2026.

> **Objective:** to understand what artificial intelligence is, where it comes from, and what distinguishes an agentic system from a simple automation.

This stage starts at the beginning, taking nothing about the subject for granted. By the end of it you will not know how to build a system; you will know what is being talked about when people talk about artificial intelligence, what can be expected and what cannot, and — the most useful part of all — when none of it is needed.

## By the end of this stage you will be able to

- Explain, plainly and precisely, what a language model does and what it does not do.
- Tell apart three things that are confused every day: a classifier, a generator and an agent.
- Place where all this comes from, with three milestones and their consequence, without heroic storytelling.
- Decide in which cases a problem is **not** solved with artificial intelligence, and argue it.
- Describe what changes and what does not change for those who develop software.

## Prerequisite

The craft of programming, at the level described in the [starting point of the path](../../camino/00-punto-de-partida.en.md): editor, terminal, Git and some Python or JavaScript. Nothing about the subject is assumed. Pages 1, 2, 3 and 5 do not require writing any code.

## Exit criterion

Submit a written analysis of three processes of your own, saying in which of them artificial intelligence is not needed, by which criterion you discard it, and what you would use instead. The exercise and its template are in the [stage exercise](practica.en.md). Without that analysis, the stage is not closed.

## Pages

1. [What artificial intelligence is](01-que-es-la-inteligencia-artificial.en.md) — an operational definition, what “model”, “training” and “inference” mean, and what can be expected from a system of this kind.
2. [From Turing to AlexNet: the history up to 2012](02-historia-hasta-2012.en.md) — from Turing and Dartmouth to the winters, expert systems, the statistical turn and computer vision. Every milestone with its technical consequence.
3. [From AlexNet to agents: the history since 2012](03-historia-desde-2012.en.md) — deep learning, transformers, the generative stage and the agentic one. It closes by stating what this atlas covers and what it leaves out.
4. [What an agent is and what it is not](04-que-es-y-que-no-es-un-agente.en.md) — the boundary between automation, assistant and agentic system, with the criterion that tells the three apart.
5. [Where artificial intelligence is not needed](05-donde-no-hace-falta-ia.en.md) — the negative criterion: eight disqualifying conditions, the alternatives, and forced agentification as the first anti-pattern.
6. [The craft in 2026](06-el-oficio-en-2026.en.md) — what changes and what does not for those who write software, with the available evidence and its limits.

## Notes on the stage

- **Pages 2 and 3 carry the heaviest research load in the project**: every milestone has its primary source, checked with an HTTP request before being cited, and its consultation date. When the canonical edition sits behind a paywall, the original is cited and an open copy is linked beside it, saying which is which.
- **No heroics.** History is told through its technical and economic consequences, not through its protagonists: the AI winters explain as much as the summers.
- **The 2012 break** is not a matter of pagination convenience: it separates two different ways of working, the one that writes knowledge down and the one that learns it from data.
- **What cannot be supported is not asserted.** Page 3 states expressly that there are no consolidated public figures on productivity, and page 2 warns that the savings attributed to 1980s expert systems come from testimony, not from auditable accounts.
